*This is a sample research entry — a working note published to demonstrate
the format, not a finished piece of research.*

Most frameworks for a good life treat health as one priority competing with
others: career, relationships, meaning, fun. The Eshkere thesis starts from a
different observation — health is not a competitor in that list. It is the
surface the list is written on.

## Non-optional means non-optional

You can opt out of chess, of status games, of ambition. You cannot opt out
of needing sleep, food, movement, connection and a mind that works. When
health degrades, it does not politely shrink one line item; it taxes every
pursuit at once. That asymmetry is why we call it the floor.

## The floor is not the point of the building

Treating health as foundational is not the same as treating it as the goal
of life. Nobody builds a house to admire the foundation. The point of
securing the floor is everything you get to build on top of it — which is
where the second half of the thesis, *play games*, takes over.

## Open questions this note does not settle

- Where exactly is the line between a health need and a chosen pursuit?
- How should limited attention be split between protecting the floor and
  playing above it?
