Software engineers talk about technical debt: shortcuts that make this week
faster and every future week slower. The same accounting applies one layer
down, to the body and mind doing the engineering.

Skipped sleep, skipped meals, skipped friendships — each one is a loan taken
out against the foundation. The interest doesn't arrive as one dramatic
bill. It arrives as a small tax on everything: slower thinking, thinner
patience, games that used to feel vivid going flat.

The point of the metaphor is not guilt. Debt is sometimes worth taking on —
briefly, knowingly, with a repayment plan. The failure mode is the quiet
kind: borrowing against health by default, for games that were supposed to
be the reward.
