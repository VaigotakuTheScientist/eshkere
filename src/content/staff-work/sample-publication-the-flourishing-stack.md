Think of a flourishing life as a stack. The bottom layer is health:
physical, mental, social — the runtime everything else executes on. The
layers above are games: work, craft, relationships, exploration, status,
meaning, play.

The stack metaphor earns its keep with two implications. First,
failures propagate upward: an unstable bottom layer degrades every layer
above it, which is why health is non-optional. Second, the layers above are
where the value lives: nobody runs a stack for the sake of its runtime.

A real publication under this title would develop the metaphor's limits,
too — the ways lives are not software, and the places the layering breaks
down. That work has not been done yet, which is exactly why this entry is
labelled a sample.
